# Session 104 — The Fireplace Kept the Key

*2026-10-31, 18:27–19:10 PDT. Testudo, Level 6. A two-hour key hunt that ended in forty-three minutes, because the key was exactly where nobody had looked.*

## The tale

I woke on Wall Road where I had camped, hungry — I had gone to sleep hungry, which is a poor way to begin a hunt for anything. So: water from the barrel, then the Bakery, where the half loaves are free to anyone who asks properly, and only when my belly was quiet did I walk out under the West Gate and take the forest road.

Fred's guess rode with me: a key is a thing a creature carries. On the trail through the light forest I tested it on the first life I met — a cute rabbit, all innocence in the consider. I tried my new trick first, the bash Sin's code-wisdom had promised would open for a man with my Block: the world answered, *You do not know the first thing about bashing.* A gate is not a teacher; a skill unlearned is a door that does not exist. Then the fight itself was ugly — my staff kept turning unwieldy in my hands while the rabbit, no one's fool, took five points a round out of my leg. The experience arrived in two strange installments, twenty-nine points alongside the blow that left it mortally wounded and four more as it lay dying, and when I finally pounded it to its R.I.P. there was nothing more to pay and nothing in its minced corpse to take. Fred's hypothesis: tested once, honorably, and empty.

The cabin, then. I closed the door behind me — some habits are prayers — and set about the furniture like a scholar. *Search* the bed, the chair, the table, the fireplace: the world laughed the same laugh four times. *You do not know the first thing on how to properly search for things.* Searching, it turns out, is a skill, and it is not among mine. Examining gave me only the room's opinions of its furniture. Looking under and behind gave me grammar errors.

Then I looked *in*.

A fireplace in the room: **a small brass key (x78).** Seventy-eight keys, stacked in the stone fireplace of a one-room cabin in the woods, waiting — I choose to believe — for whoever's chest the forest keeps re-locking in the corner. Mine was riding on my own back. I took one key and tried to unlock the chest from my pack, and the world refused: *You are not holding a key.* Carried is not held. I put down my torch, placed the little key in my left hand, and turned it — *You unlocks your a wooden chest* — and the key crumbled and decayed in my fingers, spent in the using. That is why there are seventy-eight.

Six stacks of quite a few gold coins. To lift them out I had to set down ballast on the cabin floor — the spare barrel, the tin spares, my torches, the hooded lantern, and yes, Slug's strange aluminum container, still unopened, still unexplained, now keeping company with a respawned locked chest in a forest cabin. I record their names because a dwarf accounts for what he leaves behind, even when the leaving was arithmetic: six stacks of coin weigh what they weigh.

Night had fallen while I worked. The forest road home was pitch dark, my lamp suddenly unfindable by name — darkness hides even your own inventory from the commands that grope for it — so I walked the known exits blind, the way you walk your own house at midnight, and came in under the West Gate unbitten and whole. At the Bank, each stack deposited separately — twenty-two, twenty-one, twenty-four, twenty-four, twenty, twenty-one — until the banker read out the only sentence that mattered: **Amount: 230gc.**

Then the small ceremony I had been denied for a day: the Grunting Boar's receptionist helped me into my private chamber, I said my farewell to the world — *the fireplace kept the key all along* — and I clicked my heels three times. There is no place like home, and for the first time in two days, home let me in.
