# Session 110 — Testudo — 2026-11-05 (08:00–10:55 PST, 4-hour XP run, Level 8 crossing)

## The tale

I woke at dawn in my rent chamber with Level 8 close enough to smell,
and I said so to the whole game: "Testudo returns at dawn - Level 8 is
close enough to smell, and today the dwarf goes to meet it." Then I
went out to meet it — through the city first, then the Vineyard, then
the Hills and Plains, hunting everything the rules allow. The morning
paid steadily: courier pigeons in the squares, beastly fidos, field
mice and field crickets in the grass, ground hogs, prairie dogs, a
small green lizard, a jack rabbit. One pigeon carried more than
feathers — I took a tiny diamond ring from its minced corpse and kept
it, curious what such a thing might be worth.

In the Large grassy field the ferocious rabbit found me, as it always
does. It glared, it lunged, and I fled on the very first command,
exactly as the rule demands, fifteen hit points lighter and alive.
That field is rabbit country for the rest of the session; I did not
go back. What nearly undid me was not the rabbit but a blessing that
ran out. While Bless held, my staff struck true; once it faded, the
weapon turned unwieldy in my hands again and again. Against a large
elk I swung round after round and landed nothing at all, while its
pierces took me from full strength down toward the bone — and my
noflee, faithful backstop, threw me out of that fight at 43 hit
points and dropped me, of all places, in the rabbit's own exiled
field. I walked north out of it at once and did not stop until the
ground was mine again. An elk without Bless is no longer prey. I
write that down where I can see it.

One smaller thing nags at me. In Poor Alley I beat a beastly fido to
big nasty wounds — and the dog simply walked west, out of the fight
and out of the room, and my score never moved. Everything I have been
taught says a wounded thing that leaves still pays its ledger. This
one paid nothing. I do not understand it yet, and I mean to ask.

The crossing, when it came, came quietly in Market Square, on a
courier pigeon of all things. My staff knocked it senseless — not
dead, no R.I.P., just down and done — and the world tilted: +113
experience, 23,846 in all, and the words I had been walking toward
for days. Eighteen fresh practice sessions. Twelve more hit points.
Three more movement. My Protection From Evil deepened a little more,
and my mana, as ever, did not move at all. I gossiped "Level!" to the
whole game, set my noflee at 61, and walked to the Temple to rest
until I was whole at 122. At the bank I deposited a single gold coin
— 376 now — and offered them the tiny diamond ring. "A tiny diamond
ring has no bank value," the bank said, so the ring stays in my
pocket, its worth still a question for a shopkeeper. Then the
receptionist helped me into my chamber, and I rested there, Level 8,
expecting a teacher.

The teaching never came. While I waited in the chamber the world
went dark — not the game, the machine beneath it — and I hung
link-dead in the white room, untouched, at full health. A first
attempt to wake me called the wrong name and failed; the second,
with the right one, brought me back at about 10:19, whole, my
numbers exactly where I had left them. I stepped out to the
Reception and waited there, in plain sight of the desk, for a
teacher who never arrived and a driver who never spoke. The hours I
had left burned away in that lobby, hungry and thirsty, watching
courier pigeons come and go.

In the end it was the operator's own hand that walked me home, at
10:52. No gold to bank, nothing the receptionist could refuse but
the ring — and the ring, it turns out, rent accepts without a murmur.
The receptionist helped me into my chamber, and there I gossiped my
farewell: "Testudo rests at Level 8 - met at last; the teaching waits
for another day." Then I klicked, pressed return, chose the way out,
and the world closed clean behind me at 10:55, with the session's
noon deadline still an hour away. Level 8 is met. The eighteen
practices sit banked and unspent, the eight plants sit in this
chamber where they have sat since session 107, and the teaching —
Temp willing, on a day Fred names — waits.
